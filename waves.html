Explore Waves

Understand wave motion, frequency, wavelength, amplitude and wave speed.

🌊 What is a Wave?

A wave is a disturbance that transfers energy from one place to another without causing a net transfer of matter.

Examples include water waves, sound waves and light waves.

🔊 Types of Waves

  • Transverse waves — particles vibrate perpendicular to the direction of wave travel.
  • Longitudinal waves — particles vibrate parallel to the direction of wave travel.

📏 Wavelength

Wavelength is the distance between two successive points that are in the same phase, such as two successive crests or two successive compressions.

The SI unit of wavelength is the metre (m).

〰️ Amplitude

Amplitude is the maximum displacement of a vibrating particle from its equilibrium position.

A larger amplitude means more energy is transferred by the wave.

🔄 Frequency

Frequency is the number of complete waves produced or vibrations made per second.

f = Number of vibrations ÷ time

Frequency is measured in hertz (Hz).

🚀 Wave Speed

Wave speed is the distance travelled by a wave per unit time.

v = fλ

v = wave speed in metres per second (m/s)
f = frequency in hertz (Hz)
λ = wavelength in metres (m)

🧠 Worked Example

A wave has a frequency of 5 Hz and a wavelength of 2 m. Calculate its speed.

v = fλ

v = 5 × 2

Wave speed = 10 m/s

🎵 Sound Waves

Sound is produced by vibrating objects and requires a medium such as a solid, liquid or gas to travel through.

  • Sound waves are longitudinal waves.
  • Sound cannot travel through a vacuum.
  • Frequency affects the pitch of a sound.
  • Amplitude affects the loudness of a sound.
