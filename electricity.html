Understanding Electricity

Learn about electric current, voltage, resistance and circuits.

⚡ What is Electricity?

Electricity is associated with the movement and interaction of electric charges.

In an electric circuit, charged particles can move through conducting materials when there is a suitable source of energy.

🔌 Electric Current

Electric current is the rate of flow of electric charge through a conductor.

I = Q ÷ t

I = current in amperes (A)
Q = charge in coulombs (C)
t = time in seconds (s)

🔋 Voltage

Voltage, also called potential difference, is the energy transferred per unit charge between two points in a circuit.

  • Its SI unit is the volt (V).
  • A battery can provide a potential difference.
  • Voltage provides the energy that drives current around a circuit.

🧱 Resistance

Resistance is the opposition to the flow of electric current in a circuit.

  • Its SI unit is the ohm (Ω).
  • A higher resistance generally means less current for the same voltage.

📐 Ohm's Law

For a conductor under constant physical conditions, the current is proportional to the potential difference.

V = IR

V = voltage in volts (V)
I = current in amperes (A)
R = resistance in ohms (Ω)

💡 Worked Example

A circuit has a voltage of 12 V and a resistance of 4 Ω. Find the current.

I = V ÷ R

I = 12 ÷ 4

Current = 3 A

🔗 Simple Electric Circuit

A basic circuit can contain a source such as a battery, connecting wires, a switch and a component such as a lamp.

  • Battery — supplies electrical energy.
  • Wires — provide a conducting path.
  • Switch — opens or closes the circuit.
  • Lamp — converts electrical energy into light and heat.
