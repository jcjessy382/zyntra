🔢 What is Algebra?

Algebra uses letters and symbols to represent unknown or changing numbers.

In algebra, letters such as x and y are called variables. Numbers that are fixed are called constants.

3x + 5
Example

If x = 4, then:

3x + 5 = 3(4) + 5
= 12 + 5
= 17

✏️ Algebraic Expressions

Learn how to identify and simplify expressions.

🔤

Like Terms

Like terms contain the same variables raised to the same powers.

3x + 5x = 8x
➕

Simplification

Collect like terms to make an expression simpler.

4x + 3 + 2x + 5 = 6x + 8

✨ Expansion

Expansion removes brackets by multiplying each term inside the bracket.

3(x + 4) = 3x + 12
Worked Example

Expand:

2(x + 5)

Multiply 2 by every term:
= 2x + 10

🧩 Factorisation

Factorisation is the reverse of expansion.

🔍

Common Factor

Find the greatest common factor of the terms and take it outside the bracket.

6x + 12 = 6(x + 2)
🧠

Difference of Squares

A difference of two squares can be factorised using a standard identity.

a² − b² = (a − b)(a + b)

⚖️ Linear Equations

Solve equations by keeping both sides balanced.

2x + 5 = 15
Step-by-Step

2x + 5 = 15

Subtract 5 from both sides:
2x = 10

Divide both sides by 2:
x = 5

⚡ Laws of Indices

Indices are powers that tell us how many times a number is multiplied by itself.

Multiplication

aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ
Example

2³ × 2²
= 2⁵
= 32

Division

aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ
Example

5⁴ ÷ 5²
= 5²
= 25

Power of a Power

(aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ
Example

(2³)²
= 2⁶
= 64

Zero Index

a⁰ = 1
Example

7⁰ = 1

📈 Quadratic Equations

A quadratic equation contains a variable raised to the power of 2.

ax² + bx + c = 0

📋 Important Algebraic Identities

These identities are useful for expansion and factorisation.

Identity Formula
Square of a sum (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b²
Square of a difference (a − b)² = a² − 2ab + b²
Difference of squares a² − b² = (a − b)(a + b)

🚀 Quick Revision

Remember these key ideas before your exam.

Variable: A letter representing an unknown or changing value.
Constant: A fixed numerical value.
Like terms: Terms with the same variables and powers.
Expansion: Removing brackets by multiplication.
Factorisation: Writing an expression as a product of factors.
Linear equation: An equation where the highest power of the variable is 1.
Quadratic equation: An equation where the highest power of the variable is 2.
